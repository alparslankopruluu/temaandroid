# Distribution — creators, meme pages, UGC, paid ads

*Read when executing the MRR ladder beyond rung 1 (`docs/growth-plan.md`): buying posts,
recruiting creators, producing UGC, or — only at rung 3 — paid ads.*

## Boundary (reference, do not restate)

Every external action here follows the approval/no-auto-spend/disclosure boundary in
`docs/playbooks/post-launch.md` ("Approval and publishing boundary"): nothing in this
playbook authorizes spend, publishing, DMs at scale, or persistent commitments without a
fresh, exact approval. **Every sponsored or creator post carries an explicit #ad /
paid-partnership disclosure — non-negotiable**, and the ethics bans (no planted/seeded
comments, no incentivized reviews, no purchased engagement/followers, no fake discovery)
apply to every tactic below. All dollar figures in this file are starting anchors to
record against as evidence, never promised outcomes.

## Meme-page buying (rung 2)

- Economics to expect and record: ~$20–50 per post for ~20–40k views in most niches — a
  $0.50–1.00 CPM. Cheap because no personal brand is attached to the repost; you are
  buying a targeted audience without paying for a personality.
- Vetting: real conversation in the comment sections (emoji walls suggest purchased
  engagement), consistent posting history, audience/niche match with the ICP.
- Track results per page; a page that converts is a repeat channel, one that doesn't is
  a one-time test. Log actual CPM and revenue per post as evidence.

## Influencer SOP (rung 2 → 3)

**Prospecting by feed engineering.** Open a fresh account and behave exactly like the
ICP: search what they search, watch their tutorials to the end, like and comment as
them. Within days the feed is a self-refreshing prospect list — every surfaced creator
is relevant by construction. Before messaging anyone, picture the actual video: how
would this specific person show the app, in their format, without it feeling like an ad?
If you cannot picture it, skip them regardless of their numbers.

**Green/red flags.** Followers do not matter; views and engagement do. Green: ≥25k
average views with a floor that never dips under ~15k, performance across platforms, a
distinctive style, comment sections with real conversation. Red: wildly inconsistent
views, trend-hoppers, emoji-only comments, big views with near-zero comments.

**DM structure.** Three parts: open with "Paid promo?" (money is the only opener that
reliably earns a reply in a flooded inbox), one sentence on what the app does, a clear
invitation. The one-sentence description doubles as idea validation — if the app cannot
be explained in one sentence to someone being paid to care, it will not survive five
seconds with a stranger scrolling past.

**Volume calibration.** Founders give up after fifty DMs when the channel typically
needs hundreds to produce information. Messages are written and sent by a human —
never automated, never mass-blasted — and follow-ups across channels (DM + email +
warm introduction) are normal sales practice, not spam. Keep it to people whose
content the offer genuinely fits.

**Negotiation.** Get off DMs and onto a call as fast as possible; text dies and voice
closes. Open warm and specific about their actual content. Anchor at a $2–3 CPM
computed on deliberately underestimated average views, structured as a flat monthly
number with a minimum-view guarantee (they keep posting until they hit it) and a
per-video cap so one viral hit doesn't break the budget. Frame it as a partnership
("$X/month for N videos with a view minimum"), not a commission. Pay 20–50% up front,
the rest on completion; a weekly payday on recurring deals quietly lowers effective CPM
because views keep accruing after the tracking window. Keep an objection log — after
~20 calls the same three or four objections repeat, and the pitch can answer them
pre-emptively.

**Contract clauses up front, in writing:** the ad-usage right (permission to run their
content as paid creative later — nearly free when asked at signing, expensive
afterward; never run someone's face in an ad without it) and the disclosure requirement
(#ad / paid partnership on every deliverable).

**Creative direction.** The creator is the hero; the app is a tool in their hand.
Study their best-performing format and put the app inside it (day-in-the-life, routine,
educational, Q&A) rather than imposing an ad format. Push one gotcha feature per
creator until conversions dip (~5–10 posts), then introduce the next feature — see the
channel-decay clocks in `docs/playbooks/post-launch.md`.

## UGC (rung 1 → 2)

Curiosity-hook plus honest demo carries a UGC video — not charisma, not a following,
not a face. Faceless variants (screen recordings, static-image posts, build-log posts)
are legitimate. Real product footage rules per `docs/playbooks/marketing-video.md`;
the video must demonstrate shipped behavior only.

## Paid ads (rung 3 only)

- **Gate: only after organic creatives already convert and the audience is known.**
  Ads scale a working machine 2–3×; they do not fix a product, and if ARPU clears CPM
  by a hair the result is a machine for laundering money into an ad platform. Paid
  marketing is a tax on defensibility — growth ends the day the spend stops.
- **Creative over targeting:** broad targeting with a strong creative beats narrow
  targeting with a weak one; the first hundred ad tests are creative tests. Reuse
  proven creator content (with the ad-usage right above).
- **Ad-library forensics:** research competitors in public ad-transparency libraries
  (e.g. Meta Ads Library) filtered toward the longest-running / most-backed ads —
  nobody keeps paying for an ad that doesn't return. Ask why each works; remake the
  mechanic, never copy the creative.
- **Cashflow:** Apple pays out roughly 1.5 months after the customer charge — the ads
  reinvestment cycle must budget for that gap.
- Every campaign follows the one-experiment discipline and spend-approval gates already
  defined in `docs/growth-plan.md` and `docs/playbooks/post-launch.md`.

## Saturation and decay

One gotcha feature per creator until conversions dip, then the next feature. Whole
tactics decay over years; the founders still standing assume decay is coming and test
the next channel while the current one still works.
